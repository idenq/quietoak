Frequently asked questions

Does Steadygut diagnose IBS or tell me what to eat?

No. It is a diary. It shows what you logged and what often came before your flare-ups, so you can discuss it with your doctor or dietitian.

What counts as a flare-up?

A severe symptom, Bristol type 1 or 7, or blood noted in a bowel movement. Entries within 6 hours belong to the same flare-up.

Where is my data?

Only on your iPhone. There is no server, and an account is optional (Sign in with Apple keeps your Apple user ID and name on the phone). Share a PDF or CSV from the Report tab when you want to.

What is free?

All logging, history, reminders and CSV export. Pro adds Insights, trends and the PDF report.

Subscriptions

  • Cancel: iPhone Settings → your name → Subscriptions → Steadygut Pro → Cancel Subscription. Cancel at least 24 hours before the renewal date to avoid the next charge.
  • Refunds are handled by Apple: reportaproblem.apple.com.

Your data

Steadygut needs no account (Sign in with Apple is optional), has no servers, no analytics, no ads and no tracking. Your diary is stored on your iPhone and leaves it only when you share a PDF or CSV yourself. Read the privacy policy.

Steadygut is a personal diary, not a medical device, and not medical advice. It does not diagnose, treat or prevent any condition and does not recommend diets or medication. Patterns it shows are correlations in your own entries, not causes. See a doctor promptly for red-flag symptoms such as blood in stool, unexplained weight loss, fever, symptoms that wake you at night or severe pain, and talk to your doctor before changing your diet or treatment. Sources: Bristol Stool Form Scale (Lewis & Heaton, Scand J Gastroenterol 1997); red-flag symptoms (NICE guideline CG61; ACG Clinical Guideline on IBS, 2021).
